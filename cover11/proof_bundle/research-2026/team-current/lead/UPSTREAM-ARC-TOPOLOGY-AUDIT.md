## Finite Voronoi vertices have positive sectors, even when cocircular
For a finite vertex x with distinct incident sites c_j at common distance rho>0, the local nearest-site comparison along x+tv is exactly maximizing (c_j-x).v over incident sites. Nonincident sites have a strict distance gap locally. Taking v=(c_j-x)/rho gives strict maximum for j, since all other distinct points of the same circle give rho cos(theta)<rho. Thus each incident cell has an open positive-width sector; sites are in strictly convex cyclic position. Consecutive cells meet along a positive-length ray locally, and no cell repeats in the link. There is no zero-width-sector exception requiring repeated dual nodes or pseudo-polygons. Cocircularity instead gives a genuine simple polygonal dual face, which can be triangulated retaining the original common witness.
Together with connected boundary ownership and distinct switches, the spoke/collar construction in DUAL-DISK-INTERFACE-AUDIT gives a simple embedded dual disk with nine outer vertices and two interior hubs. It does not assume original trivalence. Artificial triangles retain a real common covering point.
