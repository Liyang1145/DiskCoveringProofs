# k10 separated/duplicate hubs reduce to one-hub capacity

Scope fixed radiusU, inner-root normalized ring disks10, inactive hubs h1,h2 with |hj|<1−U, core K=B(0,1−2U) covered by hubs. This is a reduction, not a completed k10 exclusion. It is conditional on a certified one-hub weighted-circle exclusion uniform over |h|≤3U−1.

## Separated or tangent hubs, d=|h1−h2|≥2U
If d>2U, the disks are disjoint closed sets with positive separation. Since connectedK is covered by their union, K lies wholly in one disk.
If d=2U, the disks meet only atp. K\{p} is connected (a nonempty planar disk minus at most one point is path connected); its intersections with the two disks are disjoint relatively closed sets covering it. Each is also relatively open as the complement of the other, so one is empty. Closure gives K contained in one disk.
Thus one hub h obeys |h|+(1−2U)≤U, i.e.|h|≤3U−1.

Every boundary point x ofB(h,U) except perhaps the single tangency point is strictly outside the other hub. Because both inactive hubs lie strictly insideD, this circle lies inintD. Move a small distance outward from h; such points are outside BOTH hubs, hence covered by the10 ring disks. By their finite closed union, x itself is covered by the rings. Closure also covers the possible tangent point. Therefore the10 ring disks cover bothS1 andS(h,U), exactly the two-circle necessary condition used in the k11 dual.

Any nonnegative two-circle measure with total demand>11 times maximal one-ring capture also excludes10 rings. Accordingly a uniform one-hub k11 dual certificate excludes this entire d≥2U k10 branch, including tangency, with no clipping/arc-intersection singularity.

## Duplicate hubs, d=0
Merge identical hubs. The core lies in that disk, so|h|≤3U−1. The same outward-limit argument shows its full circle must be covered by10 rings. The same one-hub inequality excludes this branch as well.

## Remaining domain
Only0<d<2U requires genuine two-hub exposed-union analysis. Near d=0 the two-center formula can still be ill-conditioned; merging is exact only atd=0, not a neighborhood. No neighborhood exclusion is asserted here without interval bounds.
