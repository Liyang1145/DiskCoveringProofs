# Initial literature source check (not exhaustive, no novelty claim)

Tool: Exa web search query `covering a disk with twelve equal disks optimal radius 12 circles 0.361103 proof lower bounds Voronoi`; followed by full fetch of URLs below in the initial n12 research round.

1. https://erich-friedman.github.io/packing/circovcir/
Title Circles Covering Circles. Normalization is unit covering disks and largest known target radius. Current fetched table: n12 r=2.769+, Found by Hans Melissen in1997. n11 also Found, n10 Proved by Gábor Fejes Tóth in2005. This table's printed precision cannot distinguish the saved C182 from the algebraic comparator. It does not certify optimum or establish present novelty.

2. https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2651575/what-is-the-exact-value-of-the-radius-in-the-six-disks-problem
Question by Tito Piezas III, posted2018-02-15; accepted answer now displayed as Parcly Taxel,2018-02-16. The Ed Pegg update is in the QUESTION, not the accepted answer. Exact fetched wording:
`Ed Pegg brought to my attention that n=12 ... has r(12) ≈ 1/2.769 ≈ 1/(x+1) ≈ 0.361 where x is a root of the simple x^3-2x-2=0, hence is again solvable in radicals. Compare to the symmetric but non-optimal r_s(12) ≈ 0.372.`

Important source correction: the displayed relation is approximate, not an equality, and there is no supplied n12 geometric derivation proving that this algebraic number is the radius of the illustrated layout. Therefore lit2/pegg_n12.md's strong reading as an exact public best-known configuration value is not independently established by this quote. The algebraic number itself is an exact mathematical comparator; numerical improvement against it need not mean a new best construction. This provenance issue affects the historical comparison clauses of C182/C191, not the C182 fixed-configuration upper bound.
