# Covers to the topological dual disk (n14 k=10/11), interface by interface

New product of `research-2026/next-cover-global/members/topology/`. Generalises the read-only
`n12-global/lead`-side K9 dual construction to `N = 10` or `11` rings and `m <= 4` hubs, with two deliberate
changes required by the lead: (i) **no straight-line realisation is claimed or needed**; the certificate only
needs incidence plus common witnesses, so a *curved/topological* dual suffices; (ii) the outer boundary is
proved by a **collar of radial arcs**, without assuming that the ring sites form the outer layer of the sites'
convex hull.

Conditional inputs (not re-proved, not re-audited here): (K1) boundary ownership connected, ten (resp. eleven)
switches — `lead/BOUNDARY-OWNERSHIP-PROOF.md`; (K2) no chords: non-adjacent ring **disks** are disjoint
(strictly stronger than cells-disjoint; only the implication is used) — structure member, main measure argument
lead-reviewed, `r <= U` canonical, same-arc inner-root reused; (K3) C1142: `k in {10,11}` for `r <= U`;
(K4) coverage of D at radius r by 14 congruent disks.

**Branch bookkeeping (corrected).** `N` is the number of boundary-active disks, so:
k=10 ⟹ `N=10` and `m = #distinct hub centres in {3,4}` (four hub disks, two of which may coincide);
**k=11 ⟹ `N=11` and `m=3` exactly** (`14-11 = 3` hub disks, `>=3` distinct by the core count, hence no merging).
Consequently the `N=10, m=3` catalogue (93 entries) is the k=10 branch with a merged hub pair, and does **not**
cover k=11; the k=11 branch needs its own `N=11, m=3` catalogue (85 entries), generated by the same enumerator.

## 1. Cellulation facts (same as n12 with N rings)

Inner-root replacement (read-only) makes all sites distinct points of int D with ring norms in
`[1-r, sqrt(1-r^2)]` and hub norms `< 1-r`; merging coincident hubs keeps positive cells (`s in int V_s`).
Under (K4) every clipped cell satisfies `V_s subset B(s,r)`; by (K2) non-adjacent ring cells are disjoint.
Under (K1) exactly `N` boundary vertices (switches) exist, each of degree 3: two boundary arcs and one
positive-length **spoke**; each spoke's inner endpoint is an interior primal vertex (n12 Lemma 1.3, count-free).
At an interior primal vertex at most two ring cells meet and then they are adjacent (else (K2) fails); hence
every face of the dual contains at least one hub, the only ring-ring facets are the `N` spokes, and the ten
spoke faces are the only 2-ring faces. The hub graph `H` is connected (face-adjacency label argument).

## 2. The curved dual `G^c` (no straight-line embedding)

* Nodes: one per cell, placed at the site itself (`p_s = s`; `s in int V_s`).
* For every facet `phi = V_a cap V_b` (a positive-length segment) pick its **midpoint** `q_phi`.
* Edges: the two straight segments `[p_a, q_phi]`, `[q_phi, p_b]`, both lying inside the convex cells.

*Lemma A (embedded).* In one convex cell `V` with interior point `p`, the segments from `p` to the midpoints
of distinct facets are pairwise disjoint except at `p`: each ray from `p` meets `∂V` in exactly one point, so
distinct facets give distinct rays and the two segments share only `p`. Distinct cells have disjoint interiors
and two cells share at most one facet (convexity of their intersection). Hence `G^c` is an embedded plane
graph (its edges may be piecewise straight; no straight segment between two *sites* is asserted anywhere).
QED

*Lemma B (bounded faces are vertex stars).* Each interior primal vertex `x` has an empty ball: with
`rho = |x-s| <= r` for the incident sites and `|x-t| > rho` for all others. The incident cells in cyclic order
around `x` give a bounded face whose boundary walk passes through the facet midpoints at `x`; conversely every
bounded face arises this way. The incident sites lie on the circle `∂B(x,rho)`, are distinct, and no three of
them are collinear; hence they are in strictly convex position in the cyclic order around `x`, the star polygon
is convex and can be fanned, and **every fan triangle carries the common witness `x`** with `|x-s| <= r` for all
its vertices. This is the only "convexity" the certificate needs: an empty ball, not a hull assumption. QED

*Lemma C (outer boundary is the ring cycle, via the collar of radial arcs).* For a ring cell `V_i` let `I_i` be
its owned boundary arc, `s_{i-1}, s_i` the two spokes and `q_{s_{i-1}}, q_{s_i}` their midpoints. The closed
curve
`C_i = [w_{i-1}, q_{s_{i-1}}] u [q_{s_{i-1}}, c_i] u [c_i, q_{s_i}] u [q_{s_i}, w_i] u arc(I_i)`
is simple (the two radial segments are distinct rays from `c_i`, the two spoke halves lie on distinct spoke
lines, and `arc(I_i) ⊂ ∂D`; only the intended endpoints are shared, using `c_i ∉` either spoke line), hence
bounds a topological disk inside `V_i`. Gluing the `N` disks along the spoke halves `[w_i, q_{s_i}]` (shared by
`C_i`, `C_{i+1}`) gives a topological **annulus** (collar) whose outer boundary is `∂D` and whose inner
boundary is the closed polyline `gamma = u_i ([q_{s_{i-1}}, c_i] u [c_i, q_{s_i}])` through all `N` sites and
spoke midpoints (a simple closed curve by Lemma A). Every dual segment from a site `c_i` towards a hub facet
leaves `c_i` on `gamma` into the disk bounded by `gamma` and cannot meet `gamma` again (distinct rays), so all
hub cells, all hub nodes and all bounded faces lie strictly inside `gamma`. Therefore the outer face of `G^c`
has boundary walk exactly the ring cycle `R_0 R_1 ... R_{N-1}`, the hub nodes are interior, and the bounded
faces (the vertex stars of Lemma B) tile the disk bounded by `gamma`. QED

**Consequence (disk + counts).** `G^c` is an embedded disk: one outer face (`N`-cycle) and one bounded face
per interior primal vertex, each a convex star, fanned with common witnesses. Euler gives `F = N + 2m - 2`,
`E = 2N + 3m - 3`, interior edges `N + 3m - 3`, `N` outer triangles and `2m - 2` inner ones, and each hub edge
in exactly two triangles; the enumerator's catalogue is exactly the finite list of fan triangulations of these
disks (degenerate cocircular faces are absorbed by the same empty-ball fan; fanning a face from a hub never
adds a ring-ring edge because a 2-ring face is a spoke face whose pair is already adjacent). **No straight-line
embedding of the sites, no convex-hull-outer-layer assumption, and no genericity assumption is used.**
