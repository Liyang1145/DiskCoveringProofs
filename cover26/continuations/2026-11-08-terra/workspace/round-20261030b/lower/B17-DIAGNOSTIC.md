# Diagnostic: b=17 area versus B=17 radial constraints

At r=0.2273 and 0.228, a numerical finite-circle linear relaxation was tested using the following **additional integer occupancy**: for physical B=17, the remaining nine disks must cover the core |x|≤1−2r; one disk covers the origin and cannot meet its boundary because r<1/4; at least eight disks must meet that core boundary by its maximum arc length. Hence exactly one center lies in [0,r], eight lie in [1−3r,1−r], and seventeen lie in [1−r,1+r], allowing overlapping endpoints. A three-group weighted angular LP with circles both smaller and larger than r obtained only a zero objective gap at both tested radii. This is a numerical relaxation outcome, not a proof of an actual covering and not a statement about the Voronoi owner count b.

At r=0.2273, b=17, δ=π/17, the unrestricted Jensen-plus-triangle area upper bound exceeds π numerically by approximately 0.0013463. In the symmetric lens-end configuration with all δ equal and d=cosδ−sqrt(r²−sin²δ), the switch triangle area is `(1−d cosδ)d sinδ`; its deficit from r²/2 is approximately 0.0000427445 per switch, or 0.00072666 over 17 switches. That particular equal-angle coupling would still leave approximately 0.0006197 above π. The figures are diagnostic only: they neither prove the optimal coupled deficit nor exclude nonsymmetric angle configurations.

Any proof that actual B≤16 would give Voronoi b≤16 only after establishing that every boundary owner is one of the physically boundary-intersecting disks. In a strict counterexample argument the disks may first be enlarged and perturbed; B and b must refer to that same enlarged/generic realization. No such B=17 exclusion is established here.
