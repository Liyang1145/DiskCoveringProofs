## Statement

Let a true at-most26-disk cover at rho<=U=234701/1000000 be represented by
the current cyclic boundary owners and an embedded triangular disk on its
physical centers (or a limit of such embeddings). Suppose a closed present
subcomplex triangulates a triangle with consecutive boundary-center labels
i,i+1,i+2 (indices moduloB). Suppose this closed patch contains ALL already
introduced nonowner vertices, and there are at most THREE unintroduced
physical nonowners remaining. Then this entire geometric source prefix,
including every possible continuation outside the closed patch, is impossible.

The patch condition is not inferred from a face count alone. Its distinct
triangular faces, used vertices, edge incidences, Euler characteristic and
connected interior/boundary vertex links must certify a triangulated disk
whose ONLY boundary is that three-cycle. In any nonoverlapping straight-line
embedding it fills the geometric triangle: its interior vertices lie in the
convex hull of those three boundary centers. This also holds in limits by
closedness of the convex-hull inclusion. An arbitrary rod realization need
not satisfy this embedding condition and is not an admissible substitute.

## The entire filled ear misses a fixed half-disk

Rotate the unit anchor shared by owners i and i+1 to A_i=(1,0), and write
A_(i+1) for the next anchor. Both c_i and c_(i+1) have distance at mostrho
from A_i. Center c_(i+2) has distance at mostrho from A_(i+1).

Every owner's short footprint has angular length below1/12turn when
rho<1/4. Indeed an arc of length at least1/12turn has endpoint chord at least
2sin(pi/12)>1/2>2rho. Here
sin(pi/12)^2=(2-sqrt(3))/4>1/16, because3<(7/4)^2.
The existing short-footprint/cyclic-ownership ingredient ensures these are
the short arcs, not a returning or major-circle interval.
Thus the gap from A_i to A_(i+1) is below1/12turn, so

A_(i+1).(1,0) > cos(pi/6)=sqrt(3)/2 > 4/5.

Consequently c_i.x,c_(i+1).x>=1-rho, and
c_(i+2).x>4/5-rho>=4/5-U.
Their convex hull, and EVERY introduced nonowner in the filled ear, have
x>=h=4/5-U=565299/1000000>U.

## Four distinct outside-ear nonowners are required

Set R=1-2U. The previously written core-circle coverage argument shows that
the nonowner disks cover the CLOSED disk of radiusR: boundary-owner disks
miss its open interior, and the finite nonowner closed union contains its
closure. This is still true if a boundary owner tangentially meets its circle.

Consider four target points, in the same rotated frame:

O=(0,0), L=(-R,0),
P=(-R/2,+sqrt(3)*R/2), Q=(-R/2,-sqrt(3)*R/2).

All lie in the closed core, all have x<=0, and every pair is at distance
at leastR. Since R>2U>=2rho, no one radius-rho disk covers two of them.
They require FOUR DISTINCT nonowner disks. None can be centered inside the
filled ear, so at least four nonowners must lie outside it. At most three
unintroduced nonowners remain, a contradiction. The argument covers the
ENTIRE angular domain and ALL future outside-ear continuations, not a point,
a proper neighborhood, or a numerical stress suggestion.
