# Six separated core targets outside a filled boundary ear

This strengthens the earlier four-target lemma. It uses the SAME original
boundary-owner and embedded-center/corner-shadow premises, not a sampled
cover or a new nearest-site diagram of shadow labels. No formal current
payment or unrestricted n26 completion is asserted here.

Set U=234701/1000000, R=1-2U, h=4/5-U. The bound closed-ear projection
argument places every center in a filled consecutive-owner triangle in
x>=h, relative to its shared unit-circle anchor and inward radial frame.
The original nonowners cover the closed core disk of radius R.

Take SIX targets, with all coordinates exact:

O=(0,0),
A+=(R/2,sqrt(3)R/2), A-=(R/2,-sqrt(3)R/2),
B+=(-R/2,sqrt(3)R/2), B-=(-R/2,-sqrt(3)R/2),

The five nonzero targets are five vertices of a regular hexagon with its
rightmost vertex omitted. All targets lie in the core. Every pair is at
distance at least R, and R>2U. Thus any radius<=U disks covering the targets
must use SIX DISTINCT ORIGINAL nonowner disks.

Every target has x<=R/2. Any center in the ear therefore has horizontal
distance at least

h-R/2 = (4/5-U)-(1/2-U) = 3/10 > U

from every target. Consequently no ear disk can cover any of them. Six
distinct ORIGINAL nonowners outside the filled patch are required.
This projection comparison is stronger than restricting targets to x<=0;
the two positive-x targets are still strictly inaccessible to ear disks.

The topology test certifies a complete closed triangular subdisk in the
